Helping fund essential infrastructure for a growing community

Development Cost Charges (DCCs) are fees collected from new developments to help pay for the infrastructure needed to support growth, such as roads, water, sewer, drainage, and parkland. These charges reflect the capital costs that new development places on municipal services. The amount of DCCs can vary depending on the condition and capacity of existing infrastructure, the development’s location, land use type, and specific project characteristics.

The Village of Pemberton first introduced a Development Cost Charge Bylaw in 1995. While that bylaw was not adopted at the time, a revised version (Bylaw No. 521) was adopted in 2004 following approval from the Inspector of Municipalities. This bylaw applied DCCs to subdivision approvals and, in some cases, building permits.

Charges for the Village Core supported upgrades to water and wastewater systems, road construction, and parkland acquisition. In the Industrial Park, DCCs supported water and wastewater improvements.

In 2013, the bylaw was updated to provide greater clarity around the capital projects and DCC rates for different land uses including, residential, commercial, institutional, and industrial. Charges were generally reduced, except for industrial uses, which saw an increase due to the addition of road and drainage costs.

Development Cost Charge Bylaw No. 723, 2013 (Consolidated)

When Are Development Cost Charges Payable?

Development Cost Charges are required when:

Subdivision approval is granted; or
A building permit is issued for construction or significant alteration of a building or structure.

Fees are calculated according to Schedule A of the DCC Bylaw and are payable at the time of subdivision approval or building permit issuance.

Exemptions

Development Cost Charges do not apply in the following situations:

The building is exempt from taxation under provincial legislation
The building will have fewer than four self-contained residential units and no other use
The value of work under the permit does not exceed $50,000 (or the amount set by regulation)
The permit is for construction of self-contained residential units each under 20 m², used solely for residential purposes
Additional Development Charges

Alongside Development Cost Charges, other fees may apply, including:

Road frontage charges
Utility connection charges
Application processing fees
Need More Information?

For detailed estimates or help determining applicable charges for your project, please contact our Development Services team.
