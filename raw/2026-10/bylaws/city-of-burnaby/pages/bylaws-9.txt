Adoption of new Zoning Bylaw

About this project

The City of Burnaby has completed a comprehensive updated of our Zoning Bylaw. The new Burnaby Zoning Bylaw 2026 was adopted on June 9, 2026, replacing the former Burnaby Zoning Bylaw 1965. The new Zoning Bylaw went into effect on July 1, 2026.

This update modernizes and simplifies our development regulations for all zoning districts, as well as implements new provincial legislation and City plans and policies.

The objectives of the update were to:

Provide flexible and modern regulations
Make zoning accessible and easy to comprehend
Improve ease of use for applicants and City staff 
Implement and align with policies and programs

The Zoning Bylaw is a tool for regulating land use. It communicates technical requirements about use, density, and location of development in Burnaby. 

While the Zoning Bylaw was periodically amended in response to ongoing development needs, this was the first comprehensive review and update completed in over 60 years.

The process

The Zoning Bylaw Rewrite was coordinated with several key City projects and provincial initiatives, including the Burnaby 2050 Official Community Plan (OCP) update and new provincial housing legislation. As a result, the phases of the Zoning Bylaw Rewrite project were aligned with the key milestones of these initiatives. 

The Zoning Bylaw Rewrite was implemented in 4 phases and completed in spring 2026. Throughout the process we consulted with subject matter experts and residents, sometimes through the OCP process.

Click the graphic above to see or download a larger view.

Project phases
Phase 1: Small-Scale Multi-Unit Housing (SSMUH)

This phase involved the project initiation and background work being undertaken by staff to prepare for the project, including reviewing new legislative housing requirements. In response to provincial legislation, we developed and implemented new requirements for small-scale multi-unit housing, implemented through the R1 District in June 2024.  

Phase 2: Residential, Commercial & Height Framework

As part of Phase 2, we established a new height-based development framework. It supports legislated changes to inclusionary housing, community benefit bonusing and transit-oriented area requirements.  It also introduced interim versions of our new multiple-family residential and commercial districts which create a more transparent, user-friendly development framework that provides clarity on building height and form. These changes were adopted in June 2025. Learn more about the height-based framework.

Phase 3: Public, Employment, Industrial & Agriculture

During this phase, we built on the height-based framework and new multiple-family residential and commercial districts to develop the remaining new zoning districts for employment, industrial, institutional, parks, and agricultural uses that align with the City’s new OCP land use designations. These changes were adopted in March 2026. 

Phase 4: Supporting Regulations & Implementation

In this last phase, we reviewed and updated our supplementary regulations, including administrative and parking and loading sections. These updates, along with all the other new regulations completed through earlier project phases, were repackaged into a new Zoning Bylaw 2026 document. This work was followed by a full repeal and replacement of the Zoning Bylaw adopted on June 9, 2026, with an effective date of July 1, 2026.
